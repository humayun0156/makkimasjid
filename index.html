About Us

Makki Masjid Tokyo exists to serve the spiritual and social needs of Muslims in Tokyo. We strive to strengthen faith, build community bonds, and offer a welcoming place for worship and learning.

Who We Are

Makki Masjid Tokyo is a community-based mosque that:

  • Conducts five daily prayers
  • Holds Friday (Jumu'ah) prayer
  • Organizes Islamic lectures and gatherings
  • Supports Tablighi Jamaat efforts
  • Provides Islamic education through Makki Masjid Gakuin

Prayer Times

Ramadan Programs

  • Taraweeh: Performed every night after Isha prayer throughout Ramadan. We aim to complete the recitation of the entire Qur'an during this month.
  • Khatam Taraweeh: The completion (Khatam) of the Qur'an will be announced in advance — a special night of dua and gratitude.
  • Iftar: Arranged daily at Maghrib time. All fasting individuals are welcome to break their fast together at the masjid.
  • Eid Prayers: Special congregational prayers on Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha.

Makki Gakuin

Makki Masjid Gakuin is an Islamic learning institution operating under Makki Masjid Tokyo. It provides structured Islamic education for Muslim children growing up in Japan.

Our goal is to raise confident and practicing Muslim children in Japan.

Our Focus

  • Qur'an learning
  • Islamic knowledge
  • Adab (manners)
  • Akhlaq (character building)

Educational Approach

  • Age and level-based curriculum
  • Qualified and experienced teachers
  • Regular assessments and feedback
  • Strong parent-teacher coordination

Details: gakuin.makkimasjid.jp

Divisions

  • Hifz Division: Full Qur'an memorization, Tajweed and revision system
  • Nazera Division: Fluent Qur'an recitation, correct pronunciation and Tajweed
  • Islamic Studies: Aqidah (belief), Fiqh (Islamic practice), Sirah (Prophetic biography), character development

Visit & Etiquette

Welcome to Makki Masjid Tokyo. As the central Markaz for the community, we serve as a place of worship, guidance, and unity. To ensure a harmonious environment for our congregants and our Japanese neighbors, we ask all visitors to adhere to the following protocols.

Transport & Parking (Strict Policy)

  • No on-site car parking: There is absolutely no parking available for cars at the Masjid.
  • Use coin parking: If you must drive, please use nearby commercial "Coin Parking" lots.
  • No illegal parking: Do not park on the street, in front of houses, or block driveways. Illegal parking leads to police complaints and damages the reputation of the Masjid.
  • Priority for commuters: Brothers living nearby are requested to walk to the mosque. Please leave the limited bicycle spaces for those traveling from long distances.

Facility Usage

  • Prayer space: Open 24 hours for daily Salah, with a clearly marked Qibla and clean Wudu facilities.
  • Women's prayer area: We currently have no facilities for women at this location. We recommend that local brothers facilitate prayer spaces at home for their female family members with proper Parda.
  • Visiting Jamats: As this is the Tokyo Markaz, all Jamats arriving from other mosques must contact us in advance to coordinate their stay and avoid space conflicts.

Etiquette & Civic Responsibility

  • Quiet zones: Lower your voice immediately upon approaching the Masjid. Do not speak loudly on mobile phones outside.
  • Footpath etiquette: Do not gather or loiter at the entrance before or after prayers. Leave the area promptly so pedestrians have an uninterrupted footpath.
  • Cleanliness: Littering is strictly prohibited. Please keep the Masjid and surrounding streets clean.
  • Modesty: Dress modestly. Please remove shoes and place them neatly on the racks before entering the prayer hall.

Non-Muslim Guests & First-Time Visitors

We warmly welcome those who wish to learn about Islam. To ensure we can provide you with a proper welcome and a member of the community to assist you, please contact us via phone or email beforehand to schedule your visit.

Note: Your conduct at the Masjid is a reflection of your faith and your country. Let us show the best of Islamic character to our Japanese hosts.
